## 1. Key Papers

### 1.1 Foundational (Multilingual Models)

| Paper | Year | Authors | Description | Link |
|-------|------|---------|-------------|------|
| **mT5: A massively multilingual pre-trained text-to-text transformer** | 2020 | Xue et al. | Multilingual variant of T5 pre-trained on Common Crawl covering 101 languages. | **Unsupervised Cross-lingual Representation Learning at Scale (XLM-R)** | 2019 | Conneau et al. | Trained Transformer MLM on 100 languages; +14.6% on XNLI. Shows 15.7% gain for Swahili and 11.4% for Urdu over prior XLM. ACL 2020. | [arXiv:1911.02116](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116) |
| **BLOOM: A 176B-Parameter Open-Access Multilingual Language Model** | 2022 | BigScience Workshop | Decoder-only Transformer trained on ROOTS corpus (46 natural + 13 programming languages). Competitive on multilingual benchmarks. | [arXiv:2211.05100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05100) |
| **LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models** | 2023 | Touvron et al. | 7B-65B models trained on public data. LLaMA-13B outperforms GPT-3 on most benchmarks. | **No Language Left Behind (NLLB)** | 2022 | NLLB Team | Scaled MT to 200+ languages using SMoE; 44% BLEU improvement over prior SOTA. Flores-200 benchmark and toxicity evaluation across all languages. | [arXiv:2207.04672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04672) |

## 2. Techniques Summary Table

| Technique | Description | Papers | Suitability |
|-----------|-------------|--------|-------------|
| **Cross-lingual transfer (zero-shot)** | Train on high-resource (English), generate in target language without target training data | mT5, XLM-R, BLOOM | Works for related languages; degrades for distant languages |
| **Cross-lingual transfer (few-shot)** | Provide target-language examples in prompt | BLOOM, LLaMA, GPT-4 | Strong gains for low-resource; diminishing returns beyond 5-shot |
| **Machine translation + fine-tune** | Translate English stories to target language, then fine-tune | TinyStories→Arabic (Boughorbel), Multilingual TinyStories | Cheap but inherits MT errors and cultural bias |
| **LLM-assisted synthetic data** | Prompt capable LLM (GPT-4, Sarvam-M) for in-language stories | Javanese/Sundanese (Pranida), Unnatural Instructions | High cultural fidelity; needs careful prompt design; may lag in coherence |
| **Combinatorial prompting** | Systematic variation of story elements (character, setting, plot) | Multilingual TinyStories | Scalable; ensures diversity |
| **Back-translation** | Target→High-resource→Target | Turkish (Yildirim-Erbasli) | Moderate quality; can propagate errors |
| **Continual pre-training** | Further pre-train on small high-quality target corpus after MT-based training | Arabic (Boughorbel) | Effective for fixing MT-induced errors with ~1% additional data |
| **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** | Retrieve relevant documents in high-resource language, generate in target | Bengali (Hossain), TraSe (Ipa) | Grounds generation; translation-centric or cross-lingual |
| **Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA)** | Low-rank adaptation of large models | Various PEFT methods (Roh 2025) | Critical for low-resource since full fine-tuning can degrade large models |

## 4. Evaluation Approaches

### 4.1 Automatic Metrics Used

| Metric | Type | Low-Resource Suitability |
|--------|------|--------------------------|
| **BLEU** | Reference-based (n-gram overlap) | Poor for creative text; n-gram mismatch penalizes valid but different phrasing |
| **ROUGE** | Reference-based (recall-oriented) | Better for summarization; limited for story generation |
| **METEOR** | Reference-based (synonym-aware) | Better than BLEU but requires linguistic resources |
| **chrF / chrF++** | Character-level | Useful for morphologically rich languages |
| **BERTScore** | Reference-based (embedding similarity) | Better semantic capture; but may have target-language bias in embeddings |
| **SBERT similarity** | Reference-based (sentence embeddings) | Good semantic matching; depends on multilingual SBERT quality |
| **COMET** | Reference-based (cross-lingual) | Strong for MT; requires reference translations |
| **Perplexity** | Reference-free | Available for any language with a trained LM; but correlates poorly with quality |

### 4.2 LLM-as-Judge

- **G-Eval** (Liu et al. 2023): GPT-4 with chain-of-thought; 0.514 Spearman on summarization. *Risk: bias towards LLM-generated text.*
- **GPT-4-as-judge** used by: Eldan & Li (TinyStories), Boughorbel et al. (Arabic stories)
- **ProverbEval** (Azime et al. 2024): Shows 50% variance from answer choice ordering in low-resource settings. Recommends: monolingual evaluation > cross-lingual.

### 4.3 Human Evaluation

- Cultural fidelity, coherence, correctness for Javanese/Sundanese (Pranida et al.)
- 108 annotators across 71 Indian regions for TALES (Bhagat et al.)
- Human evaluation of Pashto poetry (Ullah et al.)
- Inter-annotator agreement (Spearman ρ, Pearson r) used in Dahal & Fujita 2026

### 4.4 Key Eval Challenges for Low-Resource Story Gen

1. **No gold-standard references**: Creative tasks have no single correct output
2. **Metric bias**: BLEU/ROUGE favor high-resource language patterns
3. **LLM evaluator bias**: LLMs prefer their own generation patterns
4. **Cultural blind spots**: Evaluators without lived experience miss nuance
5. **Choice-order effects**: 50% variance in MC evaluations of low-resource LMs

## 5. Architectural Choices

### Encoder-Decoder (Best for Low-Resource NLG)

| Model | Parameters | Languages | Story Gen Suitability |
|-------|------------|-----------|-----------------------|
| **mT5** (Xue 2020) | Small–XXL | 101 | Best overall for NLG; **Dahal & Fujita 2026** find it competitive for Nepali |
| **mBART** (large-cc25) | 680M | 50 | **Best** in Dahal & Fujita 2026 comparison for Nepali headline generation |
| **ByT5** (Xue 2021) | Small–XXL | Byte-level | Token-free, handles any script; useful for languages with poor tokenizers |
| **BanglaT5** (Talukder 2026) | T5-based | Bengali | Monolingual T5 for Bengali; best for keyword→text in Bangla |
| **NLLB-200** (2022) | SMoE 54B | 200+ | Primarily MT, but used for story translation in Boughorbel et al. 2024 |

### Decoder-Only

| Model | Parameters | Languages | Story Gen Suitability |
|-------|------------|-----------|-----------------------|
| **BLOOM** (2022) | 176B | 46+13 code | Strong zero-shot; but massive, hard to fine-tune for low-resource |
| **LLaMA** (2023) | 7B–65B | ~20 | Strong base for fine-tuning; LLaMA-3.2-1B shows adaptability for Nepali despite no explicit Nepali pre-training |
| **GPT-4 / GPT-3.5** | Proprietary | Many | Best for synthetic data generation; expensive; API-dependent |

### Findings on Architecture

> **Dahal & Fujita (2026)** on Nepali headline generation ranks: **mBART-large-cc25 > mT5-small > LLaMA-3.2-1B > NepBERTa**, showing encoder-decoder architectures outperform decoder-only for low-resource NLG when fine-tuned.

> **Anikina et al. (2025)**: Smart prompting reduces the advantage of larger LLMs for synthetic data generation, making smaller models viable for low-resource scenarios.

> **Roh et al. (2025)**: PEFT (LoRA, prefix tuning) achieves near full fine-tuning performance with drastically fewer parameters — crucial for low-resource settings where compute is limited.

## 6. Open Challenges

### 6.1 Data Scarcity
- Only ~2000 languages have any digital presence; story corpora exist for <50
- Most low-resource datasets are machine-translated, inheriting translationese and cultural distortion
- Human-annotated evaluation sets are rare and expensive

### 6.2 Cultural Preservation
- **88% of LLM stories** for Indian cultures contain misrepresentations (TALES, Bhagat 2025)
- MT-based approaches carry over Western cultural assumptions into generated stories
- LLMs match native writers on cultural fidelity but **lag in coherence and correctness** (Pranida 2025)

### 6.3 Hallucination & Factual Consistency
- Low-resource generation is more prone to hallucination since training data is sparse
- Fact-annotated datasets for verification are absent for almost all low-resource languages (Ghorpade 2026 on Marathi)

### 6.4 Evaluation Crisis
- Standard metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) poorly suited for creative story generation
- LLM-as-judge has demonstrated bias toward LLM-generated text
- Reference-free metrics needed but underexplored for generative tasks
- Low inter-annotator agreement across different cultural contexts
- Choice ordering effects can produce 50% score variance (ProverbEval)

### 6.5 Code-Switching
- Many low-resource communities naturally code-switch (Hindi-English, Bengali-English, Spanish-English)
- Most models are trained on monolingual data and fail at code-switched generation
- Almost no benchmarks exist for code-switched story generation

### 6.6 Tokenization Gaps
- Byte-level models (ByT5) help but are computationally expensive
- Many low-resource languages lack efficient subword tokenizers
- Morphologically rich languages (Turkish, Swahili, Tamil) produce very long token sequences

### 6.7 Compute Asymmetry
- Large multilingual models (BLOOM-176B, NLLB-54B) require infrastructure unavailable in low-resource regions
- PEFT and SLM approaches (TinyStories paradigm) are promising but underexplored for low-resource story generation
- The best performing model for a language is often one no one can afford to run locally

### 6.8 Benchmarking
- No standardized benchmark for multilingual story generation
- Flores-200 exists for MT but not for generative tasks
- Most evaluations are ad-hoc and not reproducible across languages

## 7. Synthesis & Research Directions

### What Works Now
1. **Fine-tune multilingual encoder-decoder (mBART, mT5)** on small target-language story corpora — best performance-to-data ratio
2. **LLM-assisted synthetic data generation** with cultural prompting — best for cultural fidelity, needs coherence improvements
3. **Back-translation + continual pre-training** with small high-quality in-language corpus (~1%) fixes MT-induced errors
4. **LLM-as-judge** evaluation but with awareness of bias — use monolingual prompts, randomize choice order

### Most Promising Gaps
1. **Code-switched story generation** is almost entirely unexplored
2. **Reference-free evaluation** for story quality (fluency, coherence, cultural appropriateness) in languages without benchmarks
3. **Small Language Models** (<1B parameters) for on-device story generation in low-resource languages
4. **Culturally-aware controllable generation** — guiding models to produce regionally/culturally specific narratives
5. **Evaluation taxonomies** like TALES but for other cultural regions (African, Southeast Asian, Latin American)
